原：What was described in the Bibliography in The Prophet Armed as
原：Section D' is now described asThe Closed Section of The
原：Archives'. It covers only the years 1929 – 1940 and contains Trot-
原：sky's correspondence with groups and members of the Fourth Inter-
原：national and with other well – wishers and friends, his family corre-
原：spondence, household papers, correspondence with publishers, doc-
原：umentation prepared for The Dewey Commission, papers of the
原：Fourth Intemational, &c . According to Trotsky's wish, this Section
原：of The Archives was not to be opened before the year 1980; but
原：Harvard University gave the author access to it on the basis of a spe-
原：cial authorization from Natalya Sedova, Trotsky's widow. ( In refer-
原：ences to The Trotsky Archives at large, the open section of The
原：Archives is meant.)
原：The Closed Section of The Archives consists of forty – five boxes,
原：containing 309 folders with documents and correspondence. Thus
原：folders 1 – 16 contain Trotsky's family correspondence; folders 17 –
原：25, his household papers;folders 26 – 33, correspondence with pub-
原：lishers and literary agents; 34 – 35, documentation for the Mexican
原：Counter-trial; while Fourth International papers are in folders 36 –
原：40. The rest of the material is arranged according to countries, for
原：instance, folders 65 – 70 contain Trotsky's correspondence concer-
原：ning China; folders 90 – 121 refer to France; the German correspon-
原：dence is in folders 122 – 126; the British 165 – 175; folders 214 –
原：286 contain correspondence with U.S.A. ; folders 287 – 292 correspondence with the U. S. S. R. , and 293 – 309 letters to and from Soviet citizens exiled from the U. S.S. R. To this Section of The Archives some papers were added by Trotsky's widow in 1953.
